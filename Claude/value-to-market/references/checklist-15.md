# The Fifteen Challenges (Value Proposition Checklist)

The method's auditing tool. Frameworks design; the checklist quality-checks what already exists. Three sections. **The missing answers are worth more than the found ones:** every unanswered challenge is a decision the organization never made, and that the market is making in its place.

**Open with the off-the-cuff verbalization.** Before any challenge, write the current value proposition as understood off the cuff. This baseline reveals what is really in the organization's head net of the official slides. You return to it at challenge 11 to measure the distance, which is the audit's value. Diagnostic spirit: fill it in the way you take blood tests, hoping everything is fine and wanting to know if it is not.

## Section 1 - Structure

1. **Values** - which values will you truly invest money, time and reputation in, and which asset does each draw on?
2. **Premise** - who is this for, who is it explicitly not for, under what conditions?
3. **Promise** - what is the promise, levered on which core value, in well-formed terms?
4. **Proof** - how do you show the promise is kept, across all four families, with at least one costly proof?
5. **Applicability** - to whom does the value apply (reference crowd, audiences, subjects)?

## Section 2 - Dynamics

6. **The triplet** - where does the promise apply: which value, which recipient, which channel, as a single unit?
7. **Toxic customers** - who does it explicitly not apply to? (The most-skipped and most-invalidating challenge.)
8. **Motivation** - do the reference subjects want it?
9. **Opportunity** - do they meet it in a context where it makes sense?
10. **Ability** - can they understand and use it? (8-10 are the point where elegant strategies discover they have no legs.)

## Section 3 - Verbalization

11. **Rewrite** - rewrite the proposition properly, then measure the distance from the off-the-cuff baseline.
12. **Reality check** - does the USP still hold, stripped of its values? Is it consistent across touchpoints?

## The three added challenges

13. **Promise Ladder rung** - which rung does the central promise sit on, and is the climb earned?
14. **Power figure** - which power relationship and which Power Shifting figure do you execute, and do your signals cohere?
15. **Evolution triggers** - what are the written, shared firing events for value evolution, and what does the reserve pool hold?

## Scoring for `/vp-assess`

Score each of the fifteen challenges: **Green** (decided and maintained, backed by an asset or proof), **Yellow** (partial, drifting, or asserted without backing), **Red** (never decided, or the market is deciding it for you). An unanswered challenge is Red by default. Convert to a 0-100 readiness only as a summary of the color distribution, and always name the reddest structural challenge (sections 1-2 outweigh section 3). Report per section, then the single most expensive gap, then the fastest quick win.

## The six anti-patterns (flag if present)

Aspirational (values nobody invests in), delegated (written by an agency, not owned), diplomatic (softened to avoid internal conflict), rubber vocabulary (a competitor could write the identical words), challenge 7 skipped (no toxic customer), undated (no evolution triggers). The meta-pattern: using the checklist to confirm instead of to discover.

# Archetype Map

An archetype is a recognizable type defined by the cluster of jobs it hires you for, not a demographic. Coding them makes the whole ecosystem legible and stable across the later work.

**Be generous about who gets an archetype.** Customers are the default, but the same clustering builds archetypes for every actor in the ecosystem you create value for or with: suppliers, partners, channel and platform players, complementors, even regulators or communities. Map the ones that matter to the value network, not only the buyers.

**Naming rule of thumb: every archetype carries an adjective.** Name each `<adjective> <noun-category>` (the *precise* lawyer, the *cautious* supplier, the *ambitious* partner); the adjective is what makes the type recognizable and non-generic. This sets up the personas step, where the best form is an adjective plus an adjective-used-as-noun (see `v2m-tool-personas`).

## On invocation
Read `../../references/output-protocol.md`, `../../references/customer-fit.md`, `../../references/frameworks.md`, `../../references/scorecards.md`. Load `.value-to-market/context.md` and the job set from `v2m-tool-jobs-to-be-done` (run it first if missing). Ask which ecosystem actors beyond customers are worth archetyping (suppliers, partners, complementors, others).

## Procedure
1. **Cluster the jobs** into coherent archetypes: group jobs that the same kind of actor hires you for. Do this for customers and for any ecosystem actor in scope.
2. **Code each archetype** with a letter and its category, always with an adjective: `A - the precise lawyer`, `B - the cautious supplier`. Add a number for each contextual variation, each carrying its own job cluster: `A1` in a company, `A2` in an SME, `A3` in a group enterprise.
3. **Assign every job cluster** to an archetype and variation, so no job is orphaned.
4. **Place the archetypes on several axis pairs** (for example value-sought vs price-sensitivity, expertise vs autonomy, urgency vs risk, decision-power vs usage) to reveal spread, crowding and white space. Give each archetype coordinates on each pair. Mark whether each is a customer or another ecosystem actor.

## Recommendation
The recommended archetype set with codes (each with its adjective), the two or three most valuable to serve first, the ecosystem actors worth archetyping beyond customers, and the white space between them. Feeds `v2m-tool-personas`, and the codes are reused by every later customer-facing skill.

## Output
Follow `output-protocol.md`. Build:
- a tabbed branded **Excel**: one tab listing archetypes (code, category, assigned job clusters, variation, notes) and one tab of coordinates per axis pair;
- **axis-pair charts as PNG** (scatter, branded palette, one per pair) embedded in a **PDF** of the maps;
- a **Word rationale** explaining how the jobs were clustered into these archetypes and why the variations split as they do.

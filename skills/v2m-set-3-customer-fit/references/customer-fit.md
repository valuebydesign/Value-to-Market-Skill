# Customer and Fit - Jobs, the Canvas, and Verbalization

The bridge between the company strategy and the per-offer design: understand the customer as jobs, map value to those jobs, check the fit, and articulate the result. Grounded in the vault's Jobs To Be Done (the Three Levels), The Value Proposition Canvas, and the Promise Ladder.

## Jobs To Be Done

People do not buy products, they hire them to make progress. A job is the progress a customer is trying to make in a circumstance, not a demographic and not a feature. Read every job at three levels:

- **Functional** - the practical task to get done.
- **Social** - how the customer wants to be seen while doing it.
- **Emotional** - how the customer wants to feel.

Generate jobs across two axes so the set has real variety and nuance, not one flat list:

- **Audiences and cases** - run the job set per reference subject (and, in B2B, per decision-maker and user), and per concrete case or circumstance, because the same product is hired for different jobs by different people.
- **Modifiers** - stress-test each job under a constraint that changes what "good" means: under stress, on a tight time constraint, under limited resources, with limited knowledge or visibility. A job under stress is often a different job.

Sort the resulting jobs by the objective they serve, so priorities are legible:

- **Personal gain** - the individual is better off (status, relief, mastery).
- **Task** - the immediate functional outcome.
- **Business or company advantage** - the organization is better off (revenue, cost, risk, speed).
- **Greater call** - a purpose beyond the transaction (identity, belonging, mission).

The output is a structured job set: jobs at three levels, multiplied by audience/case and by modifier, tagged by objective, with the highest-leverage jobs called out.

## Archetypes

An archetype is a customer type defined by the cluster of jobs it hires you for, not a demographic. Cluster the jobs, then code each archetype with a letter and its category (for example "A - the precise lawyer"), and a number for each contextual variation that carries its own job cluster ("A1" in a company, "A2" in an SME, "A3" in a group enterprise). Assign every job cluster to an archetype so none is orphaned, and place the archetypes on several axis pairs (value-sought vs price-sensitivity, expertise vs autonomy, urgency vs risk, decision-power vs usage) to see spread and white space. The codes are stable and reused by every later customer-facing step.

## Personas

A persona is a concrete face distilled from an archetype, carrying its code. Mark each by adoption maturity (innovator, early adopter, mass, long tail) and by role and buying dynamic (primary, secondary, herd, group, pair). Name the antithetical personas: the customers most harmful to the business, to repel by design, which feed the premise's toxic customer. Personas turn the archetype map into a targeting, messaging and repelling tool.

## Adoption and validation

Adoption is audited with the MOA model per persona (Motivation, Opportunity, Ability), and every gap becomes a test: an assumption, an experiment, a suggested KPI, a pass threshold and a falsifier. The KPIs carry forward into the engineering measurement plan.

## The Value Proposition Canvas

Osterwalder's canvas, used to test fit before building. Two halves:

- **Customer profile**: the customer's **jobs** (from the JTBD tool), **pains** (obstacles, risks, bad outcomes), and **gains** (wanted outcomes and benefits).
- **Value map**: the **products and services** offered, the **pain relievers** (how they kill specific pains), and the **gain creators** (how they produce specific gains).

**Fit** is the match: every important pain has a reliever and every important gain has a creator, ranked by how much the customer cares. Fit is achieved on paper (problem-solution), in the market (product-market), and in the bank (business model). Do not claim fit for a pain nobody ranks high.

**Feature recommendation**: read the fit to recommend the product features that matter, and the ones to drop. A feature that relieves no ranked pain and creates no ranked gain is a candidate for the cut list. The output is the filled canvas as a deliverable plus a ranked, fit-justified feature set.

## Verbalization

Turn the designed value into language a startup could say out loud and a brand team can build on. Produce, in this order:

- **The Osterwalder-style value proposition sentence**: "Our [offering] helps [segment] who want to [job] by [gain creator] and [pain reliever], unlike [alternative]." Keep it one breath, specific, and free of rubber vocabulary.
- **Payoff examples per Promise Ladder rung**: write the value proposition as it would sound at each rung it can honestly reach (Solution, Value, Cultural Resonance, Higher Call), so the climb is visible and the team can pick the altitude.
- **Semantic spaces**: the word territories the brand can own and the ones to avoid (the antithesis in language), the metaphors and the vocabulary that carry the values. This is the raw material communication strategy builds on.
- **Brand and comms seeds** (feeding the Guidelines deliverable): a tone-of-voice sketch (a few do and do-not pairs), a brand personality (a short archetype read), and draft mission, vision and purpose lines derived from the Higher Call rung and the core values. Seeds, clearly marked as starting points, not final copy.

Verbalization embodies the Value Ladder and the core values; it never invents value the design did not establish. If a rung is not earned by proof, do not verbalize at it.

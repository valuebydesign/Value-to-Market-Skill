# Value Proposition Assessment

A diagnosis: fill it the way you take blood tests. The missing answers are worth more than the found ones.

## On invocation
Read `references/output-protocol.md`, `references/checklist-15.md`, `references/scorecards.md`. Ask for the current VP (or the off-the-cuff version) and the market.

## Procedure
1. Off-the-cuff baseline; hold for challenge 11.
2. 3P headline: irrelevant (no premise), weak (no promise), untrusted (no proof)?
3. Score the fifteen challenges by section, each Green/Yellow/Red; unanswered is Red.
4. **Place the VP and its claims on the value ladder.** Map the current value proposition and each public claim to its rung on the Promise Ladder (from commodity/feature up through benefit, experience, identity, and purpose). Read the shape: a VP whose claims cluster on the low rungs is shallow and easily copied; one that reaches the high rungs, if the proof backs it, is strong. Flag any claim that sits high but has no proof beneath it (aspiration masquerading as value).
5. Flag the six anti-patterns.

## Recommendation
The reddest structural challenge and its cost, the value-ladder read (strong or shallow, and any unbacked high claim), and the fastest quick win. Readiness N/100. Route to the tool that fixes the reddest area.

Follow `output-protocol.md` for the reasoning file, self-score, and next step. Always produce the branded Word report and, since this tool is list-heavy, always generate a branded Excel: a tab for the fifteen-challenge scoring (challenge, section, Green/Yellow/Red, note) and a tab for the value-ladder placement (VP or claim, rung, backed-by-proof yes/no, strong or shallow).
